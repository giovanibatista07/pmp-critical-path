# Domain Summary: Business Environment (26%)

<!-- group: Domain Summaries | order: 3 | summary: Governance, compliance, change control, issues, risk, continuous improvement, organizational change, and external change. -->

## Task 1 — Governance
- PMO types: **supportive** (low control), **controlling** (moderate), **directive** (high).
- Governance defines structure, policies, **decision rights**, **escalation paths**, phase gates, and **success metrics**.
- Steering committees decide beyond the PM's authority; act within approval limits.
- Portfolio management selects and prioritizes for strategy; programs coordinate related projects for benefits.
- Tailor governance for agile teams: guardrails, working-product reviews, a few meaningful metrics.
- Transparent decision rules (criteria, approval limits, escalation paths) prevent lobbying and rework.
- AI use on projects needs **multidisciplinary governance**: legal, privacy, security and ethics, with named owners.

## Task 2 — Compliance
- Categories: security, health and safety, sustainability, regulatory/legal, privacy, accessibility, financial reporting.
- Confirm requirements with experts → classify → assess threats → plan controls → measure (audits) → correct and prevent.
- Privacy by design, data minimization; build compliance into the Definition of Done.
- AI risks: **selection bias** (unrepresentative data), **labeling bias** (biased tags), insecure APIs, lack of transparency.
- Financial compliance: follow the organization's approval limits, procurement rules and audit trail; never misstate progress to trigger a payment.
- Ethics: disclose conflicts of interest, decline prohibited gifts, never manipulate data.

## Task 3 — Change control
- Predictive: change request → impact analysis → CCB decision → update baselines → implement → verify → communicate. Record in the **change log**.
- Types: corrective action, preventive action, defect repair, update.
- Emergency changes use an expedited, documented procedure — not no procedure.
- Configuration management controls versions of product and documents.
- Agile: new requests go to the product backlog; protect the sprint goal.
- Hybrid: one integrated impact analysis, then the appropriate control path per component.

## Task 4 — Impediments and issues
- Risk that occurs → **issue** → execute contingency plan → fallback plan if needed → **workaround** if unplanned.
- Issue log with owner, priority, due date. Prioritize by impact on the goal or critical path.
- Recurring issues → root cause analysis → preventive action.
- Escalate organizational impediments with data and proposed solutions.
- Share hard problems early with the people who can help; hiding an issue removes options.

## Task 5 — Risk
- Risk management plan: appetite, thresholds, methodology, roles, categories, probability/impact definitions.
- Identify: prompt lists (PESTLE), SWOT, assumptions analysis, brainstorming, checklists.
- Qualitative (probability × impact matrix) → quantitative (EMV, decision trees, Monte Carlo, sensitivity/tornado).
- Threat responses: escalate, avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept. Opportunity responses: escalate, exploit, share, enhance, accept.
- Owners and **triggers** make responses happen. Residual vs. **secondary** risk.
- Contingency reserve (known-unknowns) vs. management reserve (unknown-unknowns).
- Agile: spikes, risk-adjusted backlog, frequent delivery.
- Quantify reserves from analysis (EMV, Monte Carlo percentiles), not gut feel.

## Task 6 — Continuous improvement
- Retrospectives with a few owned, tracked actions; PDCA; Kaizen; DMAIC (Six Sigma).
- Use lessons learned early; update organizational process assets (templates, checklists).
- The people doing the work should help design the process changes.
- Treat improvements as experiments: measure before and after, keep what works.

## Task 7 — Organizational change
- Assess culture and readiness; evaluate impact on each group; determine actions.
- Models: **Kotter** (start with urgency), **ADKAR** (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement), Lewin (unfreeze-change-refreeze), Bridges.
- Engage middle managers as change champions; visible sponsorship.
- Address the people side: role changes (including AI-driven automation) need early, honest communication, training and support.
- Conway's law / mirroring hypothesis: systems mirror organizations — change both together.

## Task 8 — External environment
- Scan regularly (PESTLE, experts, vendor roadmaps, regulators).
- Reassess the business case when markets, interest rates, or regulations change.
- New competitors and technologies (including AI) can change what customers value. Revisit the vision, roadmap and backlog with the product owner.
- Regulatory changes: confirm the requirement with compliance experts, run impact analysis, then change control.
- Supply-chain, currency and market shocks: quantify the impact, record risks, present options to the sponsor.
- Technology end-of-life and security changes → assess, record as risks, present options.
