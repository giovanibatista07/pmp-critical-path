# Practice-Book Errata (Skill Valley)

<!-- group: Study Plan | order: 2 | summary: Which answers in the Skill Valley 540-question practice book are wrong, broken or outdated — check here before you trust a surprising key. -->
*Independent audit of the "Academy Skill Valley — PMI PMP PMBOK 7 Practice Exam Book" (3 practice exams × 180 = 540 questions). Checked Sep 29, 2026 against PMBOK 7, the Agile Practice Guide, the Scrum Guide and the July 2026 PMP Exam Content Outline. Question IDs: E1-Q052 = Practice Exam 1, Question 52.*

## Verdict

Usable for practice, but **don't trust it blindly**. 391 of 540 questions (72%) had no issues. The other 149 break down like this (one question appears in two rows):

| Problem | Questions |
|---|---|
| Answer key wrong, or contradicts the book's own explanation | 12 |
| Broken item (identical options, "(Correct)" printed in an option, missing picture/explanation, copy-pasted explanation) | 52 |
| Debatable key, or explanation teaches something inaccurate | 51 |
| Not real-exam style (True/False, "none of these", pure recall) | 30 |
| Outdated exam facts | 5 |

**Good news:** the answer letters are evenly spread (A 112 / B 104 / C 116 / D 116 on 4-option items), and the right answer is the longest option only 27% of the time. That's close to chance, so you can't game the book by picking the longest answer. Only one question is repeated (E1-Q011 ≈ E1-Q173, same answer).

## 1. Outdated exam facts (Chapters 1–2 and the exam headers)

| The book says | The real 2026 exam (since July 2026) |
|---|---|
| 175 scored + 5 pretest, 230 minutes | **170 scored + 10 pretest, 240 minutes**, two 10-minute breaks |
| People 42% / Process 50% / Business Env. 8% | **People 33% / Process 41% / Business Env. 26%** |
| About half predictive, half agile/hybrid | **About 60% agile/hybrid** (per prep providers) |
| The 2021 task list (e.g., "Manage conflict", "Lead a team") | **26 new tasks** (see *The 2026 PMP Exam at a Glance* in the app) |
| True/false items appear on the PMP (E2-Q056, E2-Q078, E2-Q138) | The PMP has no true/false items |

The book is especially thin on **Business Environment**, now 26% of the exam: governance, compliance, change control, organizational change, and external and AI changes. Use this app's Business Environment questions (156 of them) to cover that gap.

## 2. Wrong answer keys — fix these in your head

| Question | Book says | Better answer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1-Q052** Identifying stakeholders | A (wait for charter approval) | **D** (start now) | Stakeholder identification starts before or alongside the charter; waiting contradicts PMBOK and the book's own E1-Q020. |
| **E2-Q003** OSCAR coaching model | C (Choices) | **B** (the goal) | OSCAR = **O**utcome → **S**ituation → **C**hoices/consequences → **A**ctions → **R**eview. Outcome comes first; the book's own explanation says so. |
| **E2-Q022** What does NOT belong in a benefits management plan | A, D, E | **B, C** | The key lists the three things that *do* belong (benefits, measures, timeframe). The explanation says the same thing. |
| **E2-Q110** Making stakeholder expectations part of success | C (keep them engaged) | **B** (define satisfaction criteria as a project objective) | The book's explanation argues for B and says "engaged" alone isn't enough. |
| **E2-Q117** Government compliance dispute | A (withdraw/avoid) | **D** (problem-solve/collaborate) | Walking away leaves the project blocked. PMI's default is to understand the gap, share evidence and fix what's needed. |
| **E2-Q119** What the team looks at in the Daily Scrum | A (nothing) | **C** (sprint backlog) | The Scrum Guide: the Daily Scrum inspects progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapts the Sprint Backlog. |
| **E2-Q159** Choosing the first agile pilot | C (low risk, low uncertainty) | **D** (low risk, high uncertainty) | Agile pays off under uncertainty. A pilot should be low-risk *and* able to show agile's benefit. *(Medium confidence.)* |
| **E3-Q010** Product owner rejects mandatory compliance changes | A (do what the PO says) | **D** (implement compliance) | A PO can't waive mandatory compliance, and a non-compliant increment isn't "Done". The ideal PMI move (PO + compliance talk it through) isn't offered. |
| **E3-Q122** Which is NOT a risk-identification technique | B (sailboat) | **D** (risk-based spike) | The book's own explanation says the sailboat *is* an identification technique and the spike isn't. A spike reduces a risk you already know about. |
| **E3-Q147** SPI = 35 / 30 | A (1.2) | **C** (1.16) | 35 ÷ 30 = 1.167. The explanation computes 1.16, which is option C. |
| **E1-Q109** Doubling manufacturing capacity | A ("virtual team on different shifts") | *no good option* | This misdefines a virtual team (it means geographically dispersed, not shift work), and option C is a broken fragment of B. Skip it. |
| **E3-Q020** What's NOT desirable in collaborative decisions | B (involving SMEs) | *C at most* | Involving SMEs is good practice; the item teaches the opposite. |

## 3. Explanations that teach the wrong thing (even when the letter is right)

- **E1-Q118 — budget formula.** The book says project budget = cost baseline + contingency reserve. **Wrong.** Contingency reserve is already *inside* the cost baseline. **Project budget = cost baseline + management reserve.**
- **E2-Q033 — estimate ranges.** It calls −5% to +10% a "rough order of magnitude". That range is a **definitive** estimate. **ROM is −25% to +75%.**
- **Scrum events list** (E2-Q011, Q035, Q040, Q079, Q085, Q113, Q129, Q133, Q141). The boilerplate explanation omits Sprint Planning and the Sprint itself, and calls backlog refinement an event. **The real list is Sprint, Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective.** Refinement is an ongoing activity, not an event.
- **E2-Q129.** It says technical approach isn't discussed in Sprint Planning. It is: the Developers plan *how* they'll do the work.
- **E3-Q022.** It describes "fast-tracking" as free experimentation. **Fast-tracking = doing sequential activities in parallel** (for example, with a lead, which is exactly what that question uses).
- **E3-Q036.** The Crystal family team sizes are wrong.
- **E1-Q081.** It keys "on time, on budget, in scope" as the definition of success. PMBOK 7 moved success to **value and outcomes**.
- **E1-Q180.** It claims a PMIS isn't used to capture lessons learned. It can be, as a knowledge repository.

## 4. Broken items — don't worry if you "missed" these

- **The answer is printed in the options:** E1-Q126 and E2-Q113 have an option that just says "(Correct)".
- **Identical options:** E2-Q062 (C = F and B = D, and C/F is a correct definition that's marked wrong).
- **Missing picture:** E1-Q024, E1-Q118, E3-Q013. **Missing explanation:** E3-Q026.
- **Explanation copied from another question:** E3-Q049 (it reuses Q048's risk-transfer text), E3-Q009 (it borrows a WIP scenario and contradicts its own key), E3-Q016.
- **Self-contradictory option text:** E1-Q074 (says "It is OK … It is not OK." in one option), E2-Q139 (the logic is inverted).
- **71 multi-answer questions never say how many to pick.** Several even say "which *one*" and then key two or three answers (E1-Q040, E1-Q103, E1-Q155, E1-Q175, E2-Q004, E3-Q060, E3-Q066, E3-Q089). The real PMP always says "Select two" or "Select three". If you score yourself, give these half credit instead of zero.

## 5. How to use this book well

1. Take the three practice exams for **timing and stamina**, but cap each sitting at **240 minutes** to match the real exam.
2. After scoring, **check every miss against this list first**. About 1 in 45 keys is wrong, and about 1 in 10 items is broken.
3. Treat its explanations as a *starting point*. For principles and models (OSCAR, Satir, Tuckman, Cynefin), confirm in PMBOK 7 or the app's study guide.
4. It's lighter than the real exam on **Business Environment** and **complex, multi-step scenarios**. Many items are one-step definition recall. Balance it with the app's scenario, case-study and Business Environment questions.
