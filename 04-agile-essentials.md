## Agile Manifesto — the four values

We value **individuals and interactions** over processes and tools · **working software** over comprehensive documentation · **customer collaboration** over contract negotiation · **responding to change** over following a plan. (The items on the right still have value — agile doesn't mean "no documentation".)

## The 12 principles (paraphrased)

1. Satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software.
2. Welcome changing requirements, even late.
3. Deliver working software frequently — weeks rather than months.
4. Business people and developers work together daily.
5. Build projects around motivated individuals; give them support and trust.
6. Face-to-face conversation is the most efficient and effective way to convey information.
7. Working software is the primary measure of progress.
8. Sustainable pace — sponsors, developers, and users can keep it up indefinitely.
9. Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design.
10. Simplicity — maximize the amount of work not done.
11. The best designs emerge from self-organizing teams.
12. Regularly reflect and adjust (retrospectives).

## Scrum in one table

| Element | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Product Owner** | Accountable for maximizing product value; owns and orders the Product Backlog |
| **Scrum Master** | Accountable for Scrum's effectiveness; coaches, removes impediments, serves team and organization |
| **Developers** | Create the Sprint plan and the usable Increment; self-managing |
| **Sprint** | Fixed length ≤ 1 month; never extended; contains all other events |
| **Sprint Planning** | Why (Sprint Goal), what (selected items), how (plan) |
| **Daily Scrum** | 15 minutes for Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal |
| **Sprint Review** | Inspect the Increment with stakeholders; adapt the backlog — a working session |
| **Sprint Retrospective** | Inspect how the team works; plan improvements |
| **Product Backlog** | Commitment: Product Goal |
| **Sprint Backlog** | Commitment: Sprint Goal |
| **Increment** | Commitment: Definition of Done |

## Kanban

Visualize work · limit work in progress (WIP) · manage flow · make policies explicit · feedback loops · improve collaboratively. Metrics: lead time, cycle time, throughput, cumulative flow diagram (widening band = bottleneck). **Little's Law:** cycle time = WIP ÷ throughput.

## XP practices

Pair programming, test-driven development, continuous integration, refactoring, collective code ownership, small releases, simple design, sustainable pace.

## Estimating and planning

- **User stories:** "As a ⟨role⟩, I want ⟨capability⟩, so that ⟨benefit⟩." 3 Cs: Card, Conversation, Confirmation.
- **INVEST:** Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable.
- **Relative sizing:** story points, T-shirt sizes, planning poker (Wideband Delphi), affinity estimating.
- **Spikes:** timeboxed research to reduce uncertainty.
- **Definition of Ready** (optional) vs. **Definition of Done** (required quality standard for every increment).
- **Vertical slicing:** split epics into thin end-to-end stories that each deliver value.

## Prioritization

MoSCoW · Kano (basic, performance, delighters) · WSJF (cost of delay ÷ size) · value vs. effort · 100-point method · risk-adjusted backlog.

## Agile metrics and charts

- **Burndown:** remaining work; flat = no progress; rising = scope added.
- **Burnup:** completed work AND total scope — makes scope growth visible.
- **Velocity:** planning tool for the team, not a productivity target; forecast with a range.
- **Cycle time, throughput, escaped defects, customer satisfaction/NPS.**

## Leadership in agile

Servant leadership: remove impediments, coach, facilitate, protect focus, build psychological safety. Teams self-organize around **how**; the product owner decides **what** and in which order.

## Agile contracts

Fixed price per iteration · capped time and materials · incremental delivery contracts · "money for nothing, change for free" (swap scope at no cost; stop early by paying a portion of remaining value).

## Hybrid patterns

- **Parallel:** different components use different approaches (e.g., predictive hardware + agile software) with planned integration points.
- **Sequential:** predictive phases for early/fixed work, then agile delivery (or vice versa).
- **Blended:** predictive governance and milestones with agile team practices; report with burnups mapped to milestones.

## Scaling

SAFe (release trains, PI planning, WSJF), LeSS, Scrum of Scrums, Disciplined Agile. Communities of practice/guilds spread knowledge across teams.
