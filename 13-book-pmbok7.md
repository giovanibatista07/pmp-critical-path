# PMBOK® Guide — Seventh Edition (PMI, 2021)

<!-- group: Book Summaries | order: 0 | summary: Principles, the eight performance domains, tailoring and models/methods/artifacts — and how they map to the PMBOK 8 vocabulary of the 2026 exam. -->

## Why it still matters in 2026

The 7th edition changed the Guide from a *process* book into a *principles and outcomes* book: 12 principles, 8 performance domains, a chapter on tailoring, and a toolbox of models, methods and artifacts. The 8th edition (late 2025) consolidates that into **6 principles and 7 performance domains** and brings back a light process layer. **The ideas are the same ones the 2026 exam tests.** You won't be asked to recite the 7th edition's lists. You will be asked what a steward, a servant leader or a value-focused PM would do next. The **124 questions** in this app tagged *Book · PMBOK 7* test those ideas in scenarios.

## The 12 principles in one line each

| Principle | What it means in a scenario |
|---|---|
| Stewardship | Act with integrity, care and compliance, for the organization *and* society/environment. Report, don't hide. |
| Team | Build a collaborative environment with working agreements, clear roles and shared accountability. |
| Stakeholders | Engage early and continuously. Satisfaction is part of success. |
| Value | Success is outcomes and benefits, not only scope/schedule/cost. Keep checking the business case. |
| Systems thinking | A project is a system of interacting parts. Look for knock-on effects before acting locally. |
| Leadership | Anyone can lead. Adapt style to the person and situation, and motivate rather than command. |
| Tailoring | Pick the "just enough" approach, processes and artifacts for this context. |
| Quality | Build quality into processes. Prevention beats inspection. Meet acceptance criteria and fitness for use. |
| Complexity | Expect emergent behavior. Use iteration, feedback, diverse views and small experiments. |
| Risk | Manage threats and opportunities within appetite and thresholds, with owners and reserves. |
| Adaptability & resiliency | Short feedback loops, learning, absorbing shocks and recovering quickly. |
| Change | Help people through change (readiness, communication, reinforcement). Avoid change fatigue. |

## The eight performance domains — what the exam looks for

- **Stakeholders:** identify → understand/analyze → prioritize → engage → monitor. Look for "meet them early", "understand interests before acting", and the engagement assessment matrix (current vs desired).
- **Team:** shared ownership, high-performing-team traits (trust, psychological safety, open communication), servant leadership, emotional intelligence, and conflict addressed early and privately.
- **Development approach & life cycle:** choose predictive, adaptive or hybrid from product (requirements certainty, ease of change, safety/regulation), project (stakeholders, schedule, funding) and organization (culture, capability) factors. Delivery cadence can be single, multiple, periodic or continuous.
- **Planning:** plan "just enough" and progressively elaborate. Estimates are ranges with confidence (deterministic vs probabilistic, absolute vs relative). Schedule compression means fast tracking (adds risk) or crashing (adds cost). Reserves: contingency (known risks, in the baseline) vs management (unknowns, outside the baseline).
- **Project work:** keep the team focused, manage physical resources and procurements, handle new work (change control in predictive, backlog in adaptive), and capture explicit *and* tacit knowledge.
- **Delivery:** deliverables must realize value. Requirements can be elicited and evolve. Definition of done (team-wide) differs from acceptance criteria (per item). Cost of quality = prevention + appraisal + internal failure + external failure.
- **Measurement:** use SMART, leading *and* lagging indicators, and dashboards and information radiators. Know the pitfalls: Hawthorne effect, vanity metrics, demotivating targets, misuse, confirmation bias, correlation vs causation. Set thresholds so variances trigger action early.
- **Uncertainty:** ambiguity (resolve with experiments, prototypes and progressive elaboration), complexity (systems thinking, reframing, iteration), volatility (alternatives, reserves), and risk (responses, owners, reserves, regular review). Opportunity responses are exploit, enhance, share, escalate and accept.

## Tailoring (a favorite exam angle)

The steps are: **select the initial approach → tailor for the organization → tailor for the project → implement ongoing improvement** (retrospectives and lessons learned feed back in). You can tailor the life cycle, processes, engagement, tools, methods and artifacts. Be wary of any answer that applies the full standard methodology "because it's policy" without checking fit. The same goes for one that drops governance entirely.

## Models, methods and artifacts worth recognising

- **Leadership:** Situational Leadership® II (directing → coaching → supporting → delegating), OSCAR coaching (Outcome, Situation, Choices/consequences, Actions, Review).
- **Communication:** cross-cultural communication, gulf of execution/evaluation.
- **Motivation:** hygiene vs motivators, intrinsic vs extrinsic, Theory X/Y/Z.
- **Change:** ADKAR (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement), Kotter's 8 steps, Satir (late status quo → resistance/chaos → transforming idea → practice & integration → new status quo), Bridges' transition (ending → neutral zone → new beginning).
- **Complexity:** Cynefin (clear, complicated, complex, chaotic, confusion), Stacey matrix.
- **Team development:** Tuckman (forming → storming → norming → performing → adjourning), Drexler/Sibbet.
- **Methods:** alternatives analysis, business justification (payback, IRR, ROI, NPV, benefit-cost), EMV and decision trees, root cause, value stream mapping, wideband Delphi, story points, retrospectives, backlog refinement.
- **Artifacts:** business case, charter, roadmap, registers and logs, RACI, story map, burn charts, cumulative flow diagram, control chart, S-curve, stakeholder engagement assessment matrix.

## PMBOK 7 → PMBOK 8 map

| PMBOK 8 domain | Where the PMBOK 7 ideas live |
|---|---|
| Governance | System for value delivery, tailoring, PMO/sponsor appendix, change and decision rights |
| Scope | Delivery (requirements, WBS/backlog, done), development approach |
| Schedule | Planning (estimating, compression, release/iteration plans), measurement |
| Finance | Planning (budget, reserves), measurement (EVM, business value), methods (NPV, ROI) |
| Stakeholders | Stakeholder domain, communication models |
| Resources | Team domain, project work (physical resources, procurement) |
| Risk | Uncertainty domain, risk principle |

The PMBOK 8 principles also fold the 7th edition's twelve together: **holistic view** (systems thinking, complexity), **value**, **embed quality**, **lead accountably** (stewardship, leadership), **integrate sustainability** (stewardship's wider duty) and **empowered teams** (team, stakeholders, change).
