# Domain Summary: Process (41%)

<!-- group: Domain Summaries | order: 2 | summary: Planning and tailoring, scope, value, resources, procurement, finance, quality, schedule, status, and closure. -->

## Task 1 — Integrated plan and delivery approach
- Choose the approach from requirements and technical uncertainty: **predictive** (stable, high cost of change), **agile** (uncertain requirements), **hybrid** (mixed components).
- **Tailor** processes and artifacts to size, risk, and context; agree tailoring with governance.
- PM plan = subsidiary plans + baselines (scope, schedule, cost) + change/configuration plans. The **charter** is separate.
- Business case = justification; charter = authorization (issued by the sponsor).
- Estimating: analogous (fast, early), parametric, bottom-up (accurate), three-point.
- Rolling wave planning; assumption log (invalid assumption → risk/issue).
- The PM is the **integrator** — resolves conflicts between subsidiary plans.

## Task 2 — Scope
- Scope baseline = **scope statement + WBS + WBS dictionary**. Work package = lowest WBS level. 100% rule.
- Exclusions prevent "obviously in scope" disputes.
- Requirements traceability matrix links requirements → objectives → tests → deliverables.
- **Control Quality** (correctness) → **Validate Scope** (customer acceptance).
- Avoid gold plating and scope creep; changes go through change control.
- Agile: product backlog is the scope; INVEST stories; split epics vertically.

## Task 3 — Value-based delivery
- Prioritize by value and cost of delay (WSJF, MoSCoW, Kano).
- MVP and incremental releases to learn and realize value early; pivot or persevere on evidence.
- Measure **outcomes**, not outputs. Benefits owners track benefits after transition.
- Validate assumptions with experiments (prototypes, A/B tests) before scaling.
- Check the business case at gates and releases; stop or pivot when the value is no longer there.

## Task 4 — Resources
- RACI: exactly one Accountable per item. Resource histograms, calendars, RBS.
- **Leveling** (can move end date) vs. **smoothing** (within float).
- Matrix organizations: negotiate with functional managers.
- Stable, dedicated teams beat multitasking; train and pair to close skill gaps.
- Virtual and distributed teams: overlap hours, working agreements, and the right collaboration tools.

## Task 5 — Procurement
- Contract types (buyer risk low → high): FFP → FPIF → FP-EPA → T&M → CPIF/CPAF → CPFF.
- Make-or-buy: total cost of ownership, capacity, time to market, strategy, risk.
- SOW, RFI/RFQ/RFP, bidder conferences (equal information for all), weighted source selection.
- Changes follow contract change control; disputes: negotiate → ADR → litigation.
- Close procurements: acceptance, claims settled, final payment, records archived.
- Agile contracts: fixed price per iteration, capped T&M.

## Task 6 — Finance
- Cost baseline includes **contingency reserve**; budget adds **management reserve**.
- EVM (CPI, SPI, EAC, TCPI) — see the formula sheet.
- Funding limit reconciliation; S-curves.
- Project selection: NPV (preferred for mutually exclusive choices), IRR, payback, benefit-cost ratio; ignore sunk costs and weigh opportunity cost.
- Agile budgeting: cost per sprint × sprints; fund incrementally and re-check value at each release.

## Task 7 — Quality
- Cost of quality: prevention + appraisal (conformance) vs. internal + external failure (nonconformance).
- Tools: Pareto, fishbone, control charts (rule of seven), histograms, scatter diagrams, checklists, audits.
- Quality ≠ grade. Control limits ≠ specification limits.
- Prevention over inspection; quality is built in by the team (QA detects, it can't create quality).
- Agile: Definition of Done, TDD, CI, test automation, shift left.

## Task 8 — Schedule
- Dependencies (mandatory/discretionary/external/internal), relationships (FS, SS, FF, SF), leads and lags.
- Critical path, float, crashing (cost), fast tracking (risk), Monte Carlo for probabilities.
- SPI can hide critical-path delays.
- Agile: timeboxes are never extended; burndown/burnup; Little's Law; WIP limits.

## Task 9 — Evaluate status
- Work performance data → information → reports.
- Leading vs. lagging indicators; variance thresholds; trend analysis.
- Burnup shows scope growth; cumulative flow shows bottlenecks.
- Look behind green indicators: check how results were produced and whether benefits are still on track.
- Keep artifacts current before reporting.

## Task 10 — Closure
- Formal acceptance **first**, then final report, lessons learned, archive, release resources.
- Terminated projects still need closure.
- Agile: stop when remaining value doesn't justify cost; final retrospective and transition.
