## What the book is about

Harold Kerzner, Al Zeitoun and Ricardo Viana Vargas argue that project management has moved from "deliver the triple constraint" to **delivering strategic business value in a volatile, digital world**. They organise the change into **ten pillars**, each illustrated with company cases. The 2026 ECO leans the same way: Business Environment is now 26% of the exam, and finance, value and governance appear throughout. The **104 questions** tagged *Book · PM Next Generation* apply these ideas to fictional scenarios.

## The ten pillars and their exam angles

| # | Pillar | Core idea | Where it shows up on the exam |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strategic delivery capability | Every project needs a visible **line of sight** to strategy. Most failures come from barriers such as weak sponsorship, misaligned priorities, silo metrics and culture. | Process 3 value · BE 1 governance · People 1 vision |
| 2 | Humanitarian & social initiatives | Urgency, many donors and beneficiaries, local context, volunteers, and accountability to the people affected. Adapt governance without dropping it. | People 4–6 stakeholders · BE 8 external change |
| 3 | Innovative cultures | Balance **alignment and autonomy**, protect time to think, and treat projects as innovation labs. Executives sponsor experiments rather than demand certainty. | People 3 leadership · BE 6 improvement |
| 4 | Digitalization | Context-driven planning, experimenting capacity, co-creation, knowledge repositories and BI. **Dashboards show status at a glance for decisions; reports explain detail.** Virtual-team engagement and outcome-focused work. | People 7–8 · Process 9 status · BE 8 AI/tech |
| 5 | Evolving delivery skills | Structured problem solving: define the real problem, not the symptom, find the root cause, weigh alternatives against criteria, and agree who decides. Also brainstorming rules and **design thinking** (empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test). | Process 2 scope · Process 7 quality · BE 4 issues |
| 6 | New forms of project leadership | Situational leadership styles, leadership across cultures, **stakeholder relations management** (identify, interact, engage, build the relationship), servant leadership, social PM, **crisis leadership** (early warning, fast transparent decisions, frequent communication) and competency models. | People 2–6 |
| 7 | Cultural shift to the project way of working | Organizations run more and more of their work as projects, so culture, career paths and support must follow. The change has to be led and sustained. | BE 7 organizational change |
| 8 | Adaptive frameworks & life cycles | One rigid methodology is a risk. **Choose among flexible frameworks** by project characteristics, and remember that flexibility needs capable PMs ("be careful what you wish for"). Manage the **fuzzy front end** (idea screening before chartering) with gates and selection criteria. Closure covers administrative, contractual and benefits handover plus lessons. Know the new causes of failure. | Process 1 plan delivery · Process 10 closure · BE 1 governance |
| 9 | PMOs & governance | Governance in a volatile world is **flexible**: clear decision rights and speed rather than bureaucracy. PMOs are evolving into strategy and value partners, and you can **measure a PMO's ROI**. | BE 1 governance · BE 6 improvement |
| 10 | Value-driven metrics | Time, cost and scope alone fail as KPIs. Pick a few meaningful metrics with owners, including **benefits, value and intangibles**. Run independent **health checks** early and set up a metrics management program. | Process 3 · Process 9 · Process 6 finance |

## Ideas that make great exam distractors

- **"Green on time and budget" ≠ success.** If benefits or stakeholder value are at risk, the project is not healthy.
- **More metrics ≠ better control.** Too many metrics hide the signal. Pick the few that drive decisions, and keep them visible.
- **A health check is not an audit hunt.** It is an independent, early look at whether the project can still deliver its value, and its findings lead to a recovery plan.
- **Flexibility needs judgment.** Letting teams pick any framework without governance fails. So does forcing one methodology on every project.
- **The gate asks "should we continue?"**, not only "did we finish the phase?". Stopping a project that no longer fits strategy is a success of governance.
- **In a crisis, communicate early and often**, decide with the best available information, and be transparent about what you don't know yet.

## Quick self-test

1. A dashboard owner adds 40 KPIs so that "nothing is missed". What would the book's authors say? *(Fewer, decision-relevant metrics mapped to objectives.)*
2. The sponsor wants every project to follow the same 14-gate methodology. What is the risk? *(A single rigid methodology doesn't fit different risk, uncertainty and customer-involvement profiles.)*
3. What makes a humanitarian project different? *(Urgency, accountability to beneficiaries, many donors, local context, volunteer teams.)*
