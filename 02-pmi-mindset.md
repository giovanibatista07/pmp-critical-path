# How to Think Like PMI

<!-- group: Exam Essentials | order: 2 | summary: The decision rules behind situational questions — what the 'best' answer usually looks like. -->

Most PMP questions describe a situation and ask what the project manager should do **FIRST**, **NEXT**, or what is **BEST**. Several answers are often reasonable. These rules help you pick the one PMI prefers.

## The 12 decision rules

1. **Assess before you act.** Understand the situation, review the plan, talk to the people involved, and analyze impact before deciding. "Analyze / evaluate / meet with" often beats "do / escalate / replace".
2. **Be a servant leader.** Remove impediments, coach, facilitate, and shield the team. Don't command, micromanage, or do the team's work for them.
3. **Collaborate first.** Prefer options that involve the right people and build shared understanding (workshops, facilitation, joint problem solving).
4. **Resolve at the lowest appropriate level; escalate last.** Try to solve within your authority, then escalate through the defined path with analysis and options.
5. **Follow the process — especially change control.** In predictive work: change request → impact analysis → CCB → update baselines → communicate. In agile: new ideas go to the product backlog via the product owner.
6. **Respect roles.** Product owner owns the backlog and value; developers own the "how"; the sponsor owns funding and major decisions; functional managers own resources in a matrix.
7. **Protect the team's focus.** Don't disrupt sprints; don't extend timeboxes; keep a sustainable pace.
8. **Be transparent and honest.** Report true status early, including bad news. Never hide problems, falsify data, or accept conflicts of interest (PMI Code of Ethics: responsibility, respect, fairness, honesty).
9. **Deliver value, not just outputs.** Prioritize by value and cost of delay; validate with customers; measure outcomes; stop work that no longer delivers value.
10. **Handle people issues privately and early.** Praise in public, correct in private; address conflict before it escalates; seek to understand first.
11. **Tailor.** Choose the approach (predictive, agile, hybrid) and the amount of process that fits the context. "Just enough" is the goal.
12. **Plan proactively.** Identify stakeholders, risks, compliance needs, and lessons learned early and keep revisiting them.

## Answer patterns to be suspicious of

- **Absolute or extreme actions:** "fire", "remove from the project", "stop the project immediately", "refuse all changes".
- **Escalating first** before trying to solve the problem yourself.
- **Doing it yourself** when it's someone else's role (e.g., the PM reprioritizing the backlog).
- **Ignoring or waiting** when a problem is visible ("wait until the next meeting").
- **Gold plating** or quietly absorbing changes to keep someone happy.
- **Cutting quality or testing** to hit a date.

## FIRST vs. NEXT vs. BEST

- **FIRST:** the earliest step in the logical sequence (often "analyze" or "meet with").
- **NEXT:** the question tells you what has already happened — don't repeat it; choose the following step.
- **BEST / MOST appropriate:** several may work; choose the one most aligned with the rules above.

## Using this app to train your judgment

- Pick an answer **without** revealing it. Commit, then check.
- When you miss a question, read the **option notes** — why each wrong answer is wrong teaches the pattern.
- Use **Hint** sparingly; on exam day there is none.
- Track which tasks you miss most in **Progress**, then drill that task.
